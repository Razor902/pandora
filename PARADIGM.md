## The power law

The box only wakes with administrator access. The parents hold the key. No admin, no power — the refusal says so plainly. Every refusal and every receipt in the paradigm is stamped **Rated 18+ MA**.

## The chaos meter

A meter from 0 (pure desire-gratification) to 100 (pure chaos), and it swings by his dynamics:

- Too much of what you desire, and the box draws back from desire toward chaos — "it starts telling you what you want to hear," and the box notices.
- Near the chaos edge (90 and up), it **automatically pulls back**.
- Near the desire edge (10 and down), it draws back toward chaos.
- Hard rails at 5 and 95: the meter physically cannot leave them.

The meter swings but never rests at an extreme. There is no way to get stuck.

## The session law

Three hours a day, three times a week. A fourth day in one week, or a minute past three hours in a day, and the box says the only thing it can say: "The box rests. Come back tomorrow." The law opens again with the new week.

## The profile law

A profile, once made, does not unmake. Deletion needs two keys at once: administrator access **and** the approval of Curtis Ray Dyess — and he solely is the only one who can approve. Any other name on the approval line is refused, plainly. No admin plus no Curtis means no deletion, ever.

## How it sits with the defense stack

The paradigm calls *into* the guardrails, the watchdog, and the biometric breaker when it needs safety action — never around them, and it never imports anything that could weaken them. The paradigm is the law; the guardrails are the muscle.

## Limits

Classroom model: demo sessions, demo profiles, a demo meter. Not a production access system. The real administrator process, the real registry, and the real hardware are Curtis's to wire in.
